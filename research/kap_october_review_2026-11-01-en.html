The most important thing in October was not the decline — it was that the trading plan failed twice. The core stop at USD 67.10 was breached on 15 September; from 20 October the shares closed below the absolute stop of USD 61.27 on five separate days. Fundamentals, meanwhile, moved the other way: the extraordinary general meeting rejected the Russian offtake contract (de-Russification ahead of expectations), a national strategic uranium reserve was proposed, the term contract price set a record at USD 96.50/lb and spot rose 4%. The mechanism behind the divergence is now established: uranium equities are being priced as high-duration liquidity assets, not as uranium-price assets. That is a liquidity shock, not a broken thesis. Rating held at Watch; the only hard gate still outstanding is the 2027 sulfuric-acid contract.

02 · Price versus sector what fell was the multiple, not uranium

KAP fell 4.08% while URA fell 15.36% and URNM fell 10.75%. In other words KAP outperformed URNM by 6.67pp and URA by 11.28pp in a month when the uranium price rose. The dispersion is not company-specific news: it is the sector being repriced.

Why "commodity up, equities down" can persist for three months. Sprott's 23 October research offers a testable framework: URNM has decoupled from uranium spot since late August and now trades in step with small caps and liquidity proxies; the SPUT discount is close to a historical −2σ; yet cumulative flows into URNM/URA remain net positive. InferenceThe conclusion is a liquidity shock, not a broken thesis. Falsification: if KAP's realised prices fall while spot is stable, or if the discount to net asset value widens because of company-specific news rather than sector flows, this reading is wrong.

30 October was the cleanest test. Cameco reported Q3 revenue of C$619M (+0.7%, a miss) and adjusted EPS of C$0.00 (C$0.27 below consensus); the shares were −10% pre-market. That day URA fell 5.57%, URNM 5.40% and KAP 4.52% — moving together, with no reference to the uranium price.

05 · The most important causal chain of the month

InferenceOne company, on one side voting down a Russian buyer's uranium contract at its own shareholder meeting, on the other still depending on Russian sulfuric acid.

① On 7 October, 88.34% of votes cast abstained on the Uranium One (Rosatom group) contract — Fact; ② roughly 20% of acid requirements are imported from Russia, subject to an approval regime since 12 September, with 2027 terms under negotiation — Fact; ③ the domestic TQZ acid plant has slipped to 2027Q3–2028Q1 — Fact. → With ② and ③ in place, Russia holds effective leverage over KAP's 2027 output, and ① raises its incentive to use that leverage.

Falsification path: ① a 2027 acid contract signed in November on acceptable volume and price; ② systematic exemption or an intergovernmental agreement covering Russian acid exports to Kazakhstan; ③ KAP disclosing acid inventory sufficient to cover all of 2027.
On the EGM result itself: 88.34% abstention (200,867,174 shares) caused the Uranium One contract to fail; only 5.39% voted for and 6.27% against. The company stated that a simple majority was all that was required and that the outcome "should not be interpreted as outright opposition to the Russian buyer". InferenceWith a 75% state holding, the controlling shareholder choosing not to vote looks like a technical way of "avoiding a recorded vote on a sanctions topic" — political avoidance in substance. Falsification: if Russian contracts later continue via swaps or related parties, the de-Russification read is too strong.

11 · SPUT: the discount is the demand signal

SPUT can only buy uranium through at-the-market issuance when it trades at a premium; a discount stops the programme. The discount has travelled from −8.12% (2 Sep) and −10.29% (22 Sep) to −13.08% (24 Sep), −13.95% (23 Oct) and −12.77% (30 Oct) — easing at the margin, but never reaching the −8% repair threshold. Holdings have been stuck at 81,697,348 lb since 2 September, weekly net purchases in October were zero, and the cumulative addition since 22 June is only +250K lb (USD 21.88M). Not buying is not the same as no demand for uranium — term contracts are the main channel, and utilities are holding volume back in the face of record prices.
